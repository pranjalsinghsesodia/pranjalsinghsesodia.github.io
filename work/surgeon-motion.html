Markerless Surgeon Motion Capture

Markerless motion capture for minimally invasive surgery: a 7 cm stereo webcam pair, pose estimation that carries joint angles through occlusions, and monocular depth.

Question

The question

I wanted to measure how surgeons move during minimally invasive surgery, without body markers, as groundwork for studying ergonomics and fatigue. Motion capture usually needs a dedicated room or markers on the body, and neither works in an operating theatre. How far could cheap cameras and open-source models go instead?

Pose

Pose and joint angles

I started with 2D pose estimation, first OpenPose and then AlphaPose, to find the joints of each person in the frame, and calculated joint angles from the keypoints. In theatre, limbs keep disappearing behind other people and instruments. When that happens the code extrapolates the limb lines to carry the angle through the occlusion.

Before using real footage I tested it on myself, standing in the lab and mimicking surgical actions. Then I ran the tracker on recordings of real procedures, with faces covered.

Calibration

Calibration and triangulation

For depth I paired two webcams 7 cm apart and calibrated them with a checkerboard: each camera’s intrinsics and lens distortion, then the pair’s position relative to each other. After rectification, the same point sits on the same row in both images, so its depth follows from how far it shifts between them. Triangulated distances came out in metres, 95-96% accurate against reference distances measured with standard instruments. I didn’t record the range of distances that figure covers, so read it as a bench check on the rig. It applies to distance only, so it says nothing about the joint angles.

Depth maps

Depth maps

Alongside the stereo pair I used Depth Anything for dense depth maps from a single camera, in real time at about 11 frames a second. To test depth on one object, I tracked a red pen cap and estimated its distance as it moved.
